## Central Conclusion

Brief equip or inspection was not a necessary trigger for the observed carried-Cooler and CONTROL `lastAged` catch-ups in the VALID 2026-08-22 run.

In that run, P0, P1, P2, P4, and CONTROL caught up around `lastAged=8.18317` before the first recorded Phase B equip snapshot around `worldHours=8.20005`. The same groups caught up again around `lastAged=10.18312` before the first recorded Phase D equip snapshot around `worldHours=10.20007`.

This conclusion applies to those observed events under the tested Build 42.20.3 configuration. It does not establish that inspection can never have an additional effect, that every context uses the same update path, or that the same behavior is universal across runs and configurations.

Confidence: high for the limited within-run conclusion that the subsequent brief equip/inspection was not necessary for those specific carried/CONTROL catch-ups; replication is required before treating it as a general runtime rule.

## Context Timing Synthesis

### Carried Coolers and CONTROL

The four carried Cooler groups and direct player-inventory CONTROL refreshed together before inspection in both observation windows of the VALID run. Their synchronization is consistent with a broader player-inventory or vanilla Food processing opportunity independent of inspecting an individual Cooler.

The INVALID run also recorded a synchronized first-window catch-up for the carried groups and CONTROL. That is a diagnostic consistency observation only and does not raise the confidence to multiple-valid-run support.

### Ground Cooler P1G

P1G refreshed later than carried/CONTROL in the first window of the VALID run, around `lastAged=8.24505`, approximately 0.06188 game hour after the carried/CONTROL catch-up. It refreshed before its own scheduled inspection.

The INVALID run likewise recorded P1G later than carried/CONTROL in its first window. This is consistent with a context-related timing difference, but only one valid run supports the intended comparison. The mechanism and stability of the offset remain unresolved.

### Powered FRIDGE

FRIDGE refreshed later than carried/CONTROL in the first window of the VALID run, around `lastAged=8.24989`, approximately 0.06672 game hour after carried/CONTROL. It also refreshed before its own scheduled inspection.

The INVALID run recorded the same qualitative ordering, with FRIDGE later than carried/CONTROL. The VALID run ended before a complete second-window FRIDGE outcome was sampled, so neither recurrence nor a stable context-specific delay is established.

## Hypothesis Assessment

### Container inspection is necessary for vanilla Food catch-up

Classification: contradicted for the observed events.

Carried/CONTROL catch-up preceded inspection in both VALID-run windows. P1G and FRIDGE also refreshed before their own inspection in the first window. This does not rule out an additional inspection effect under other circumstances.

### Equipping a carried Cooler is necessary for catch-up

Classification: contradicted within the VALID run.

All four carried groups caught up before the first recorded equip intervention in both windows.

### A broader player-inventory or vanilla update mechanism refreshes carried Food independently of inspection

Classification: supported in limited form.

Some inspection-independent mechanism must account for the synchronized pre-intervention carried/CONTROL events. The evidence does not distinguish a player-inventory-specific path from a more general vanilla Food update path.

### Catch-up follows a periodic or threshold-based vanilla mechanism

Classification: consistent with evidence but unproven.

The VALID run shows about 2.00698 game hours from the start `lastAged` value to the first carried/CONTROL catch-up and about 1.99995 game hours between the first and second catch-ups. The near-two-hour recurrence is meaningful candidate evidence, not a confirmed period.

Only one valid run and two intervals are available. The experiment itself used approximately two-hour waits, and it does not distinguish an autonomous periodic timer from an accumulated threshold processed at the next general update opportunity.

Confidence: moderate that the recurrence is a real pattern in this run; low that the current evidence identifies a general periodic/threshold mechanism.

### Ground Cooler contents behave differently from carried Cooler contents

Classification: supported as an observed timing difference; replication pending.

P1G refreshed later in the first VALID-run window. The evidence does not establish why, whether the offset is stable, or whether ground context alone is causal.

### Powered FRIDGE contents behave differently from carried and ground contexts

Classification: supported as an observed timing difference; mechanism unresolved.

FRIDGE refreshed later than carried/CONTROL and near P1G in the first VALID-run window. A complete second-window comparison is absent, and the evidence does not isolate power, refrigerator processing, temperature state, or context as the cause.

### Sustained container selection or active UI state affects refresh independently of brief inspection

Classification: unresolved and not distinguishable with the current experiments.

NEUTRAL was selected during waiting periods according to Bart-supplied metadata. No matched experiment held one Cooler selected/active throughout a waiting interval while leaving an otherwise equivalent Cooler unselected.

## Established Findings

- In the VALID run, carried P0/P1/P2/P4 and CONTROL caught up before brief equip/inspection in both observation windows.
- Brief equip/inspection was therefore not necessary for those specific events.
- P1G and FRIDGE refreshed later than carried/CONTROL in the first VALID-run window and before their own inspection.
- Observable context-associated timing differences exist in the assessed VALID run.
- No relevant Functional Coolers or harness exception explains the timing pattern.

## Findings Requiring Replication

- The synchronized carried/CONTROL behavior.
- The approximately two-game-hour recurrence.
- The later P1G and FRIDGE ordering.
- The size and stability of the context timing differences.
- Generalization beyond the tested Build 42.20.3 calibration configuration.

## Unresolved Questions

- Which vanilla processing path performs the pre-inspection carried/CONTROL catch-up?
- Is the near-two-hour recurrence an autonomous period, a lag threshold, or a threshold processed at a later update opportunity?
- Does brief inspection have an additional effect after catch-up has already occurred?
- Does sustained selected/active state change catch-up timing relative to an unselected matched container?
- What are the complete second-window timing and recurrence behavior of P1G and FRIDGE?
- Are the observed context differences reproducible across additional valid runs?

## Evidence Limitations

## Recommended Next Empirical Question

Does a Cooler held selected/active throughout an extended waiting period show different vanilla Food `lastAged` catch-up timing from an otherwise matched Cooler left unselected in the same physical context?

This targeted comparison is likely to provide more new information than simply repeating the current brief-inspection A/B/C/D sequence. A useful future protocol should compare matched Coolers, avoid inspection during the waiting interval, preserve identical update opportunities, and sample long enough before and after the candidate two-hour threshold. Varying wait duration would additionally help distinguish a fixed period from a threshold processed at an update opportunity.

This is a candidate research question, not an accepted protocol.

## Experimental Question Versus Infrastructure

The empirical variable is sustained selected/active state versus unselected state. Automatic distribution, explicit phase markers, stronger READY logging, reliable active-state markers, and extended post-phase sampling are possible Test Engineer improvements that may strengthen reproducibility, but they are not themselves the empirical question.
