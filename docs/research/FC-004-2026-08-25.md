## Central Conclusion

Across the two valid counterbalanced FC-004 runs, sustained selected/active UI treatment determined which matched carried and equipped Cooler received continuous observable vanilla Food refresh.

In Run A, selected primary `FC004-A` had 270 distinct sampled `lastAged` values while unselected secondary `FC004-B` retained one value for the complete 4.50157-game-hour measurement. In Run B, selected secondary `FC004-B` had 270 distinct sampled values while unselected primary `FC004-A` retained one value for the complete 4.50054-game-hour measurement.

At each endpoint, the selected group's sampled `lastAged` lag was approximately 0.178 game minute. The unselected group's sampled lag was approximately 4.571 game hours in Run A and 4.549 game hours in Run B. Observable age and freezing-state fields followed the same selected-versus-stale split.

The mirrored result follows selected identity while A remains primary and B remains secondary. Fixed A/B identity and primary/secondary hand assignment therefore do not independently explain the observed split.

Confidence: high for the limited conclusion that the sustained selected/active treatment package determined continuous observable refresh under the tested Build 42.20.3 setup. This is one counterbalanced pair, not independent replication, and it does not identify the underlying vanilla mechanism.

## Treatment Boundary

The tested treatment is not selection in isolation. It combines:

- the actual player-inventory selected-container binding;
- a visible, pinned, non-collapsed player inventory;
- a continuously displayed selected Cooler context;
- unchanged equipment and contents throughout measurement.

The evidence does not distinguish which part of this UI treatment package supplies the vanilla update opportunity.

## Relationship to FC-003

FC-003 established that brief equip or inspection was not necessary for the specific carried-Cooler and CONTROL catch-ups observed before intervention in its valid run. FC-004 now establishes that sustained selected/active treatment has a strong additional effect on continuous observable refresh in a matched carried/equipped comparison.

These findings are compatible rather than contradictory:

- Selection is not necessary for every possible catch-up, because FC-003 observed pre-inspection catch-up while NEUTRAL was selected according to Bart-supplied metadata.
- Sustained selection can nevertheless determine which Cooler receives continuous observable per-minute refresh under FC-004 conditions.
- FC-004 does not reproduce a universal autonomous two-game-hour refresh. Its selected group refreshed continuously, while its matched unselected group remained stale for more than two candidate intervals.

The experiments do not identify why FC-003 carried groups received broader catch-up opportunities while FC-004 unselected groups did not.

## Hypothesis Assessment

### Sustained selected/active state affects observable Food refresh

Classification: strongly supported within the tested scope.

The update pattern reverses with selected identity across two valid counterbalanced runs while hand assignments remain fixed.

### Primary or secondary hand assignment determines refresh

Classification: contradicted as a sufficient explanation.

Run A refreshed selected primary A; Run B refreshed selected secondary B while unselected primary A remained stale.

### Fixed A/B identity or prepared contents determine refresh

Classification: contradicted as a sufficient explanation.

The continuously refreshed group changes when the selected assignment changes on a fresh equivalent setup.

### Selection is necessary for every vanilla Food catch-up

Classification: contradicted as a general rule.

The accepted FC-003 synthesis preserves inspection-independent carried/CONTROL catch-ups. FC-004 supports selection as a strong continuous refresh opportunity, not as the only possible vanilla opportunity.

### Unselected Food receives no underlying vanilla processing

Classification: unresolved and not distinguishable with the current evidence.

The exposed `lastAged`, age, and freezing fields remained stale. The experiment did not subsequently select the stale Cooler, so it cannot determine whether hidden elapsed processing was absent or deferred until a later update opportunity.

### Catch-up follows a universal autonomous two-game-hour cycle

Classification: contradicted for a universal container-independent cycle.

Neither FC-004 unselected group showed observable catch-up during more than 4.5 game hours. A periodic or accumulated threshold processed only when another update opportunity occurs remains consistent with the evidence but unproven.

### Selection alone, independently of visible/open UI state, is the trigger

Classification: not distinguishable under the current protocol.

Selection, visible container context, and sustained active UI state were intentionally held together as one treatment.

## Established Findings

- Both FC-004 runs are valid for the intended matched counterbalanced question.
- The continuously refreshed group follows the sustained selected/active treatment in both runs.
- A selected secondary-equipped Cooler can refresh continuously while an unselected primary-equipped Cooler remains stale.
- Group identity and hand assignment do not independently explain the mirrored result.
- Sustained selection is a strong observable refresh opportunity under the tested setup.
- The automatic 4.5-game-hour endpoint produced complete paired endpoint evidence without later samples.

## Findings Requiring Replication

- Generalization beyond one counterbalanced pair.
- Stability of the effect across new saves, machines, builds, sandbox settings, and different Food items.
- The magnitude and duration of stale state beyond 4.5 game hours.
- Whether the same effect occurs for unequipped carried Coolers, ground Coolers, refrigerator contexts, or non-Cooler nested containers.
- Whether the result depends on inventory visibility, pinning, container display, or selection itself.

## Unresolved Questions

- Does the stale Cooler catch up immediately when it is first selected after the waiting interval?
- Does unselected Food accumulate hidden elapsed processing or receive no vanilla processing until activation?
- Which UI or inventory event supplies the relevant vanilla update opportunity?
- How should the FC-003 approximately two-game-hour catch-ups be reconciled mechanistically with FC-004 continuous selected refresh and prolonged unselected staleness?
- Does the selected treatment change only observable refresh timing or also the timing of underlying Food simulation?

## Evidence Limitations

- The evidence consists of one counterbalanced pair rather than independent replication.
- Each assignment used a fresh save and different item instances.
- Selection was established during readiness and stabilization, so early treatment response precedes formal `BEGIN`.
- The treatment bundles selection with a visible, pinned, open inventory context.
- The harness records exposed getter state and cannot directly observe a hidden vanilla scheduler.
- The protocol ends without selecting the stale group, so deferred catch-up is not observed.
- Bart-supplied execution metadata is required to establish absence of unlogged operator interaction.
- The exact endpoint refinement was not a Git commit at execution time, although its deployed Lua source was byte-verified and later committed with the evidence.

## Recommended Next Empirical Question

After a matched Cooler has remained unselected and observably stale for a predetermined waiting interval, what happens to its Food timing and freezing fields immediately when that Cooler is selected for the first time?

A future controlled protocol should preserve the FC-004 matched setup through a fixed waiting phase, capture an authoritative pre-switch snapshot, change only the selected-container binding to the stale Cooler, and sample at high cadence immediately before and after the switch. Counterbalancing the switch direction would help separate identity and hand assignment again.

An immediate jump toward current `worldHours` would support deferred catch-up at a vanilla update opportunity. No jump or gradual progression would support different mechanisms. This question provides more new mechanistic information than an unchanged repetition of FC-004, although independent replication remains necessary before broad generalization or production decisions.

This is a candidate research question, not an accepted protocol or authorization to run another experiment.
