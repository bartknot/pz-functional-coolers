## Central Conclusion

FC-006 supports one bounded managed-to-vanilla handoff sequence for the exact tested scope: call `setAutoAge()` and then immediately reapply the final Functional Coolers-managed public `age`, `heat`, and `freezingTime` projection before vanilla resumes ownership.

In the valid Build 42.20.4 run, candidate A used that sequence. It matched reference V exactly at handoff and through three explicit vanilla `updateAge()` opportunities ten game minutes apart. Their observed age, heat, freezing time, public phase flags, and `lastAged` values remained identical.

The projection-only control U retained a `lastAged` lag of `1.0000343323` game hours at handoff. At the first update it processed a distinctly larger age/freezing interval: U age exceeded V/A by `0.0416681170`, and U freezing time was `66.6689605713` lower. U reached fully thawed state by the second update while V/A remained mutually equal and still thawing. This contrast demonstrates that the experiment could detect failure to align the stale vanilla timing state.

Within this setup, A therefore shows no observable repetition of the stale managed hour and no observable omission of post-handoff progression relative to the V reference. The exact match persists beyond the first update through all three scheduled observations.

Confidence:

- high that A and V were observationally identical and U was observably sensitive in this valid run;
- moderate that the candidate sequence achieves the required bounded handoff-continuity outcome for this exact Build, Food type, context, projected state, and explicit-update schedule;
- insufficient for a universal or build-independent production API guarantee.

## Sequence Interpretation

The tested sequence is not `setAutoAge()` alone. `setAutoAge()` first aligns vanilla timing state to current `worldHours`; the harness then reapplies the authoritative final managed public values. The final projection is necessary because Functional Coolers, not `setAutoAge()`, owns the state that must be handed back.

Reference V used `updateAge()` before receiving the same final public projection. A's subsequent equality with V indicates that `setAutoAge()` supplied an equivalent observable timing alignment for this handoff scenario without processing the stale hour into the final managed state. U demonstrates that public projection alone did not do so.

The private freezing-update cursor was not directly observable. Freezing alignment is inferred from the controlled divergent response: A and V retained exact freezing-time/phase equality, while U processed the stale interval and thawed much faster.

## Established Findings
